Major Details

A detailed look at each major and their unique aspects.

Computer Science (CS)

Overview

Computer Science is the study of how computers and software actually work. It's not just “learning to code”. You learn how to think step by step, solve problems, and build programs that are fast and smart. A lot of the apps, games and AI tools we use every day were built by people with a CS background. It is one of the hardest majors in the college because it has a lot of math and logic. But if you like solving puzzles and you don't give up easily, it can be really fun.

How long is it? For most universities, it is 5 years (1 year of preparation). The first year is general courses like math, physics and English. Some just remove the preparatory year and have it 4 years.

Subjects

  • Programming
  • Data Structures
  • Analysis and Design of Algorithms
  • Operating Systems
  • Artificial Intelligence
  • Software Engineering
  • Databases

Skills you need

  • Good at math
  • Patience, as your code will break a lot
  • Logical thinking
  • Ready to study alone and practice outside class

Skills you'll gain

  • Programming in more than one language
  • Solving big problems by breaking them down into smaller ones
  • Understanding how computers work
  • Building a real software and working in a team

Career Paths

  • Software Developer
  • AI / Machine Learning Engineer
  • Game Developer
  • Researcher or university professor

Who this major fits, and who doesn't

  • You'll enjoy CS if you like math, solving hard problems, and spending hours building something until it works.
  • If you hate math or don't like sitting in front of a screen for long, CS will feel very hard.

Information Technology (IT)

Overview

Information Technology is about the tech that companies and people use every day: networks, servers, the cloud, and security. CS students build new software. IT students make sure all the systems are set up right, running well, and safe from hackers. IT is more hands-on than CS. You still learn some programming, but most of the time you're working with real systems and solving real problems.

How long is it? For most universities, it is 5 years (1 year of preparation). The first year is general courses like math, physics and English. Some just remove the preparatory year and have it 4 years.

Subjects

  • Computer Networks
  • Network Security
  • Cloud Computing
  • System Administration
  • Databases
  • Web Technologies

Skills you need

  • Interest in how devices and the internet work
  • Problem solving
  • Patience with fixing things step by step

Skills you'll gain

  • Setting up and managing networks and servers
  • Protecting systems from attacks
  • Working with cloud services
  • Fixing technical problems quickly

Career Paths

  • Network Engineer
  • Cybersecurity Specialist
  • Cloud Engineer
  • System Administrator
  • IT Support Specialist

Who this major fits, and who doesn't

  • You'll enjoy IT if you like setting things up, fixing problems, and working with real devices more than writing long code.
  • If you don't like troubleshooting or you want to build apps from zero, CS might fit you better.

Information Systems (IS)

Overview

Information Systems mixes technology with business. You learn how companies use systems and data to do their work better and make smarter decisions. An IS student is like the bridge between the tech team and the business team, because you understand both sides. You still learn programming and databases, but you also learn how to study a company's problem, plan a system for it, and manage the project.

How long is it? For most universities, it is 5 years (1 year of preparation). The first year is general courses like math, physics and English. Some just remove the preparatory year and have it 4 years.

Subjects

  • Principles of Information Systems
  • Database Management Systems
  • Systems Analysis and Design
  • Information and Computer Security
  • Internet Applications and Web Development
  • Human-Computer Interaction

Skills you need

  • Interest in both technology and business
  • Good communication
  • Organized thinking

Skills you'll gain

  • Analyzing what a business needs and designing a system for it
  • Working with databases and data
  • Building websites and simple applications
  • Managing projects and working with different teams

Career Paths

  • Systems Analyst
  • Business Analyst
  • Data / BI Analyst
  • IT Consultant
  • Project Manager

Who this major fits, and who doesn't

  • You'll enjoy IS if you like technology but also like talking to people, planning, and understanding how businesses work.
  • If you only want deep programming and nothing about business, CS is probably a better choice.
